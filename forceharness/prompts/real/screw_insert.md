Turn the green screw into the threaded hole at the center of the purple gear, all the way in.

The green screw is a green slotted knob with a grey threaded shank below it. The center hole on the wooden table is the target; the hole is threaded and smaller than the holes of the earlier plugging task. The other holes in the wooden board, the wood-colored handle and the green handle of the drawer next to it are not targets.

At the start, judge for yourself where the green screw is now: already in the hand, stuck in one of the holes of the wooden board, or already turned into the target hole. Base this on this turn's wrist and exterior photos, the gripper width, and the hand's position and height. If it is in the hand and not yet in a hole, go straight to aligning it with the hole; if it is in another hole, first grip the grey shank firmly and draw it out; if it is already turned into the target hole (also if it is still in the hand), do not draw it out; keep turning.

Position, orientation and grasp posture go by the current observation; no coordinates or fixed trajectory are preset. The tip of the shank must be aligned precisely with the purple center hole before going down; the hole is small and the photos alone may not be precise enough, so use the actions and feedback the current condition provides to find the hole, and confirm that the shank has entered the mouth of the hole before you start turning. A threaded hole does not let the whole shank drop in: once the tip is in the mouth of the hole it is usually only about 2 mm below the surface around the hole (pressing on a flat surface hardly sinks at all), and after that it only goes further down by turning. To find the hole, move along the board while pressing lightly on it, at 2–3 N: pressing harder while moving along lets friction catch the hand and easily drags the screw askew between the fingers; as soon as the tip sinks, the system stops on the spot, thrusts down with about 15 N for two seconds to press it into the mouth of the hole, and then judges whether it is in the hole; that moment of entering the hole is the most critical, and pressing down separately afterwards does not help. When the tip meets the mouth of the hole it is usually suddenly about 1 mm deeper than its surroundings; set the threshold for judging that it is in the hole to about 1 mm. The first aim at the hole is often 5–10 mm off; set the range (radius) for finding the hole to 12–15 mm, not too small. Find the hole only by moving along the board in contact, not by the point-by-point probing that "rises, moves over, goes down": in practice that is too slow and often goes down at the same spot again and again without finding the hole. If one pass does not find it, measure the hole position again nearby, move there and go over it again, or make the range larger. When measuring the hole position, first lower the hand to about 5 cm above the board (measuring from farther away is off by several millimeters), pick points on the board on both sides of the hole's edge and take the midpoint as the hole center; do not pick a point inside the hole (that reads the hole wall or bottom, and the wrist camera, looking at an angle, is off by several millimeters). What must be aligned with the hole is the tip of the shank, not the midpoint between the fingers: the screw's position and angle between the fingers differ every time, and the tip is often a few millimeters off. When the hand has come down to about 1 cm above the board, measure where the tip of the shank is (the lowest end of the grey shank in the wrist image) and how far it is from the hole center; first move the hand sideways by that difference so that the tip is directly above the hole center, then touch down and look for the hole in a small area nearby. Once the tip of the shank is in the mouth of the hole, this screw needs a fairly large downward force to go in: press down hard at the mouth of the hole, then turn while pressing; turning while pressing only lightly often does not catch the thread.

The thread must be turned in, not forced in by pressing: let the hand turn the screw about the vertical axis (rotate_z; if the menu has an action made for driving threads, it is better to use it). It is normally a right-hand thread: clockwise seen from above (negative angle_deg) tightens. Once the thread has caught, the screw goes down steadily as it turns, a few tenths of a millimeter per 30° (0.35–0.7 mm were measured in earlier episodes). It is normal that it does not go down during most of the first turn: the start of the thread has to turn until it meets the one in the hole before it catches; keep pressing and turning, and stop to check only if it still does not go down after a full turn. If the menu has an action that presses while rocking left and right (rocking insertion), try to use it in this task to help the thread catch: once the tip of the shank is in the mouth of the hole, rock once in place before the first turn; when it still does not go down after a full turn, do not let go, and also rock once before turning again. Use shift=0 and yaw_deg=5 and leave the force unset (default 18 N): only without the small circle does it keep rocking; with a small circle it stops rocking as soon as the hand is held. Its description and tool results are written for plugs; here not_seated, jammed or being told to raise it and turn it by 180° do not count as failures: only how far it went down matters. When the wrist is less than 5° from its limit the rocking cannot be completed: turn a little first, then rock. Do not let go before the screw has gone down 1–2 mm and the thread has really caught: let go before it catches and the screw will certainly fall over. While turning, watch whether the screw tilts between the fingers: if the shank is no longer vertical in the photos, or the sideways force keeps growing, stop first, release, grip the shank straight again and then turn; do not move the hand in the direction it is being shoved, that makes it tilt more and more. If the screw instead goes up, or the resistance suddenly increases, stop and check the direction and whether it is cross-threaded; do not force it.

Before you start turning (while the screw is still in the air and not yet in the hole), first turn the hand counterclockwise to near the wrist's limit: then the first grip can turn a good half turn (about 180°), and the thread is more likely to catch before the first release. The wrist can only turn so far, so one grip can only turn one stretch; at the end, the action is refused, and you are told how much more is possible in that direction. At the end: release (the screw stays in the hole), do not raise or move the hand away yet, and turn the hand back counterclockwise in place (as close to the wrist's other limit as possible, so that the next stretch can turn more). While turning back, the hand drops 3–5 mm by itself and drifts a few millimeters sideways, so after turning, first move_to back to the x, y where you released (see the position in the tool result of the release), about 5 mm higher than at the release, then close the gripper and keep turning. Always grip the screw at the middle of the grey shank, never at the green knob: gripping the shank gives a short lever and is steadier; when gripping again, apart from these 5 mm do not move up to look for the knob. The screw stays between the two fingers the whole time; there is no need to raise the hand to look, measure again or align again; even if the screw tilts a little after the release, closing the gripper straightens it. Only when the fingers are about to touch the board, raise the hand a little more and grip the shank higher up (still the grey shank). There is no limit on the number of grasps; repeat this cycle until it is turned all the way in.

It is all the way in when the screw no longer goes down and the knob sits against the purple gear; do not over-tighten. When finished, look again and confirm that the screw is turned into the purple center hole and stays upright in the hole after release; only being aligned, stopping at the mouth of the hole or standing in it without the thread caught does not count as done. Once you have confirmed it is done, finish with done(success=true, note="what was done"); if it is not done or cannot be confirmed, say so truthfully with done(success=false, note="the specific reason").
