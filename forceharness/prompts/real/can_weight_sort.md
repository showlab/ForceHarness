Sort by weight: put the three bottles onto the plates by weight. The heaviest goes on the white plate, the lightest on the green plate. The middle one stays where it is

The three bottles hold different amounts of water, which cannot be seen from outside, and their appearance has nothing to do with their weight; do not guess the weights from where they stand, the order you try them in or what the bottles look like. Identify the positions of the bottles and the plates from this episode's camera images yourself, and decide the order and the route yourself.
To grasp, align the gripper with the blue bottle cap and just pick the bottle up

The true weights of the three bottles are 294 g, 204 g and 160 g; which is which you only know after picking them up. In earlier measurements, the weight read when a bottle was picked up and held still was about 1.5 times its true weight (1.35–1.6 times): the heaviest reads about 440 g, the middle one about 305 g, the lightest about 240 g.
Weigh each bottle only once and decide as soon as it is picked up: close to the heaviest, carry it straight to the white plate; close to the lightest, carry it straight to the green plate; close to the middle one, put it back where it was and do not touch it again. Once you have identified two, the third is the remaining class and needs no weighing. Only when a reading falls between two classes and you cannot tell, put it back first and decide after weighing the others.

Before carrying a bottle to a plate, first raise it until its bottom is about 5 cm above the table, then move it sideways. When putting it down, the bottom of the bottle must land at the center of the plate: while you hold a bottle, the hand tilts 10–20° toward the robot, and the bottom of the bottle is about 3–6 cm farther toward −x than the cap, so align the bottom of the bottle in the photos with the center of the plate, not the cap.
