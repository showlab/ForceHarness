Peel the skin of the cucumber (or eggplant) with the peeler in the hand, in the +x direction (away from the base).

The skin of eggplants and cucumbers is very smooth, so the first stroke must pierce it first: if the blade is not yet in contact, keep this angle and go straight down until it touches, then press down hard in place so that the blade bites into the skin; only after it has bitten in, peel along +x; do not move sideways before it has bitten in, or the blade will only skid on the skin. If the blade is already in the skin (continuing from the previous episode), peel along +x right away. Peel until near the far end of the cucumber. The peeler is about 4 cm.

1. Make each stroke long: after biting in, go 5–6 cm at a time; do not try short bits one by one. The force relaxes at the end of every stroke and the next stroke has to build it up from the start; a stroke that is too short cannot build up the force. If the tool result says how much farther it can go in this direction at most, continue with that distance, even if it is shorter.
2. After biting in, press moderately; do not keep driving down: if the height in the tool result is still clearly dropping, do not add force. If the blade skids on the surface, it has not bitten in: go back to where it was and press hard once more; if you see the eggplant/cucumber being moved, reduce it. High resistance or a timeout is in itself no reason to reduce the pressure.
3. A tool result of timeout is not a failure, only that this stroke's time ran out: if the end point is still moving forward, keep peeling in the same direction.
4. The hand's angle slowly increasing is a normal effect of the load; do not turn it back, and it is no reason to stop.
5. When the skin hangs on the blade, do not raise the blade, so as not to tug the peeler loose in the gripper; keep peeling in the same direction.
6. As long as there is progress, keep peeling; there is no need to raise the blade to check after each stroke.

Do not stop because of these: the photos do not show the blade edge or the peeled skin, high resistance, a timeout, the hand's angle increasing, the gripper reading getting smaller or close to 0, not finding contact, a stroke stopped by a guard (for example torque_cap). These only concern that stroke; keep peeling in the next turn; you do not need to follow a tool result's suggestion to release and grip again.

Only finish when it has peeled to near the far end, or the tool result says it can go less than 5 mm farther in this direction at most, or the end point of 5 strokes in a row has hardly moved (less than 2 mm). In done, state truthfully how far it went, how large the resistance was and the effect visible in the photos; leave the peeler in the hand, no need to release it.
